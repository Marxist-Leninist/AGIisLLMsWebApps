3D Deterministic Path-Sum Renderer

PROBABILITY DOESN'T EXIST (pilot-wave view): there are no random numbers anywhere in this engine. Instead of Monte Carlo rays it evaluates the Feynman sum over paths directly: every point of the dish, evenly spaced, passes on the amplitude it receives from the light, and the complex phases add at every point in space.

The amplitude ψ is a one-photon QED amplitude in the scalar approximation (one field component, one frequency). By default the fog shows |ψ|² of its reflected part; tick Show direct field for the total. Where neighbouring paths have the same length their phases agree and reinforce; that is where the beam comes from. The field is computed once on a 3D grid, refined until it converges, then reused every frame.

Gold lines: pilot-wave paths (de Broglie–Bohm). Each photon follows the energy flow of its wave from wherever it starts; the apparent randomness is only our ignorance of that start. The paths always follow the total field (direct + reflected), whatever the fog shows. Averaged photon paths of this kind were measured in 2011 (Kocsis et al., Science). Paths never cross.

Approximations: scalar field (no polarisation); the mirror is physical optics (a perfect mirror, one bounce, no edge corrections), reflecting on its open side only; the reflected field is stored on a grid, so λ ≥ 0.1.

Drag to orbit, wheel or pinch to zoom, Shift+drag moves the light. Put the light on the red focus to form a beam.

Off the focus the path lengths no longer agree, so the beam defocuses.
0.1 to 0.5, so the field grid has at least 4 cells per wavelength. Shorter waves need more mirror samples. Between grid points the reflection is interpolated in magnitude and phase: exact for a single travelling wave (the beam), but where several reflected waves cross, fringes finer than the grid are smoothed (typically by 8% in brightness at λ 0.15, 14% at 0.1).
Only the global phase turns, nothing is recomputed. Without phase colour this shows the instantaneous wave, whose time average is |ψ|².
