What you are looking at

A live GPU simulation of the singular flow constructed in Finite time blowup for Navier–Stokes (the PDF). The paper builds, for every viscosity, a smooth forced solution that starts from rest and whose velocity becomes unbounded at the origin at t = 1 while its kinetic energy stays bounded (Theorem 1.1). This page reproduces the geometry, scaling laws and physical mechanism of its Section 2, and shows when the blowup happens on the timeline.

What is exact, what is schematic

  • Exact from the paper: similarity coordinates τ = q(1−η²), z = q^D η, X = r²/2q with A = ½+h, D = ½−h, 0<h<1/100; scalings uθ, uz = q^(−A)·profile, ℓr ≍ τ^½, ℓz ≍ τ^(½−h), Reθ ≍ τ^(−h), core energy ≍ τ^(½−3h); the exterior E ~ c∞ X^(−½−h) (a nearly potential vortex); pulse amplitude A_wave ≍ q^(−½−h/2) and wavelength ℓ_wave ≍ q^(½+h/2), zero angular mean, transverse to the wavevector, growing then decaying in each slot of logarithmic time v = ln(1/τ), radial wavelength shortened by the shear; compact-support cutoff at fixed physical radius.
  • Schematic: the profile shapes E(X,η), U(X,η) are solutions of the paper's profile equations and are not closed-form; here they are replaced by smooth analytic stand-ins with the same axis regularity, decay and symmetry (Gaussian-core swirl; a Stokes streamfunction ψ = q^D·U₀·X e^(−X)(η+b)(1−η²)² giving inflow at the dividing layer, axial outflow on both sides, and the small upward bias b the paper uses). The swirl gain E₀ is a visual amplitude, like the exaggeration in the paper's Figure 1.
  • Real dynamics on the GPU: 1–4 million tracer particles are integrated (RK2) through this divergence-free field every frame and splatted into a volume that is ray-marched with single scattering, sun shadowing, sky ambient and a stormy sky. Nothing is pre-baked.

Closest existing physical phenomenon

An intensifying stretched vortex — the Burgers vortex of turbulence theory, seen in nature as a tornado / waterspout funnel and in the lab as a bathtub vortex with axial outflow. Radial inflow conserves angular momentum r·uθ, so the swirl spins up as the core contracts; axial outflow removes the incoming mass so the collapse can continue; viscosity carries angular momentum back outward and the balance fixes the self-similar profile. The oscillatory pulses at the edge of the core are centrifugal (Rayleigh-type) instability waves feeding on the shear, the same mechanism as the spiral bands and suction vortices at the rim of a tornado core. The condensation funnel rendered here is the tornado analogue: it marks the low-pressure, high-speed core (drawn on the similarity variable X), and its hourglass shape is the level set X = const, i.e. r² ≈ 2X(τ+z²), whose waist pinches to a point as τ→0.

Transformer FPS regulator

Frame rate is held as high as the display allows by a small transformer that runs and trains in the page: every frame becomes a token (measured frame time, render resolution, ray steps, shadow steps, pixel count, volume size, tracer count, vsync-pinned flag); two attention blocks (d=24, 32-token history) read the sequence plus a query token carrying a candidate render level and predict that level's frame time. It learns online with Adam from the frame times actually measured (censored loss when the frame is pinned at the refresh interval), and every 8 frames the controller picks the highest-quality level whose predicted cost fits the frame budget, with a hard step-down guard and gentle upward probing when pinned at vsync. The volume size and tracer count are fixed by the preset; resolution, ray steps and shadow steps float.

Reading the display

The view frame co-moves with the collapse: it shrinks like τ^(γ/2) (γ slider; γ=0 is the lab frame, γ=1 the fully self-similar frame). Wall-clock time is warped so the apparent motion stays watchable; the physical remaining time τ and the frame's physical half-width are shown live. Speeds in the core climb as τ^(−½−h); the run stops at the chosen τ, marks the blowup at t = 1, and loops.
