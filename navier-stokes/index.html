What changed from the earlier illustration

The GPU now evolves density, all three momentum components, total energy and a passive tracer. It does not prescribe the subsequent velocity field, a shrinking radius or an infinite speed. The material is a specified dry-air approximation, not a universal fluid. The initial vortex and one bounded stirring pulse are chosen for this experiment. They are not the uploaded paper’s exact profiles or forcing.

Physical corrections actually included

Compressibility and pressure feedback: density is not fixed, pressure accelerates the fluid, and pressure waves propagate at the local sound speed c = √(γRT). Energy coupling: compression, expansion and viscous work change the gas’s internal energy. Viscosity and heat conduction: a full Newtonian shear-stress tensor and Fourier heat flux are included; both transport coefficients depend on temperature using Sutherland fits. No arbitrary speed limiter or temperature cap alters a computed state.

Viscous heating is included through conservative stress work, not added a second time as an extra heat source. The solver also accumulates an approximate ∫σ:∇u diagnostic. Finite-volume numerical dissipation can also convert resolved kinetic energy into internal energy. It is not molecular viscosity, and small conservation errors do not establish small solution errors.

Material and physical units

Initially T₀ = 300 K and p₀ = 101,325 Pa; R = 287.05 J/(kg K), γ = 1.4, ρ₀ = 1.176624 kg/m³, c₀ = 347.219 m/s. The default reference length is 100 µm and the box is 800 µm on each side. Time is reported in microseconds; the 0.720 µs window is shown in slow motion. This length is a normalising scale, not a measured core radius.

μ(T) = 1.716×10⁻⁵ (T/273)³ᐟ² ×384/(T+111) Pa·s.
κ(T) = 0.0241 (T/273)³ᐟ² ×467/(T+194) W/(m K).
The stress uses Stokes’ zero-bulk-viscosity approximation. Constant γ omits detailed molecular vibrational relaxation.

Initial condition, forcing and boundaries

The initial compact vortex combines smooth swirl, radial inflow near the middle plane and axial outflow. Density and temperature start uniform; its pressure is not artificially imposed to hold that vortex in equilibrium. Pressure readjustment is consequently part of this chosen initial-value problem. It is not a continuation of a solved incompressible reference.

The optional force is a bounded spatially compact vortex/strain-shaped acceleration, multiplied by sin²(πt/1.4) for 0 < t < 1.4 in sound-crossing units, and zero afterwards. It adds finite energy, tracked as work. The pulse is continuously differentiable at its endpoints; it is not claimed to reproduce the paper’s C∞ force. See the source for the exact deterministic initial fields and force.

The numerical domain is periodic in all three directions. Mass, tracer and total energy are conserved up to numerical rounding, with force work accounted for. Pressure waves can eventually wrap around; this is not an infinite open-air laboratory. The shaded floor is optical staging only, not a wall. Initial velocity is axisymmetric, but the calculation is on a full 3D Cartesian mesh. No nonaxisymmetric stability claim is made.

Numerical method and limits

A GPU finite-volume scheme uses primitive-variable MUSCL reconstruction with a monotonised-central limiter, a local Lax–Friedrichs (Rusanov) numerical flux, centred viscous/thermal face fluxes and two-stage SSP Runge–Kutta time integration. The state is stored in 32-bit floating point. A GPU reduction computes a timestep from acoustic/advection and diffusion rates. A failed positivity/finite-state check rejects the step and retries at half the timestep; repeated failure stops the run, rather than clipping velocity, density or energy.

A 64³ mesh is an exploratory calculation, not a fully resolved turbulence or molecular simulation. Compare 32³, 64³ and 96³ before interpreting peak values. Numerical broadening of steep gradients can outweigh molecular transport. The rendered tracer is a virtual optical marker, without particle inertia or a model of condensation. Fine shading detail is not extra numerical resolution. Select Raw computed envelope to disable the illustrative fine texture; this never changes the fluid state. Temperature and density views use diagnostic colours, not predicted light emission.

Where this implementation stops

Conservative software guardrails pause a run outside 200–600 K, above a pressure upper-bound estimate of 1 MPa, or at estimated gradient Kn > 0.02. These thresholds mark this implementation’s chosen validity window, not universal phase transitions. The gradient-length Knudsen estimate uses λ ≈ (μ/p)√(πRT/2) and resolved density, temperature and velocity gradients, with the reference length as an upper bound on the diagnostic length. It is a heuristic warning, not a kinetic simulation.

Stopping does not mean a real fluid stops. Such a run needs more complete material or kinetic physics and finer spatial resolution. This air model does not simulate liquid cavitation, bubble interfaces, ionisation, nuclear reactions, magnetic fields, gravity or relativity. It makes no claim that adding compressibility removes every mathematical singularity.

Connection to the uploaded paper

Finite Time Blowup for Navier–Stokes, §2 and Figure 1 on page 4, supply the inward-spiral/two-sided-outflow motivation. The paper’s incompressible intensity core is an Eulerian region, not a fixed mass being compressed (§3.1, page 8). Its full oscillatory forcing and residual-correction construction are not instantiated. This experiment therefore tests a finite physical analogue, not the fate of that exact singular solution.
