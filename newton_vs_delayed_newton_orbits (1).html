Newton vs Delayed Newton in a toy solar system

Left canvas: ordinary instantaneous Newtonian gravity. Right canvas: a naive delayed or retarded Newton model where each force points toward the source body’s position at t - r / cg. This is deliberately the crude toy law, not General Relativity. In other words, the whole point here is to let you watch the lag misalign the force and wreck orbits when cg is low enough. What this demo is actually doing

Units are astronomical units, years, and solar masses with G = 4π². The bodies are Sun, Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, and Jupiter in one plane. For clarity, the main interaction is Sun ↔ planet only, so planet-planet forces are omitted. That keeps the comparison readable instead of turning the page into a tiny numerical therapy session.

  • Ordinary Newton canvas: force uses the source position at the same simulation time t.
  • Delayed Newton canvas: force on each body uses the source position sampled from history at approximately t - r / cg, with linear interpolation.
  • The ghost marker and white line on the delayed canvas show the Sun position Earth is actually “aiming at” in the delayed model.
  • With realistic cg = c, the naive delay effect is tiny on this view. Lowering cg exaggerates it so you can watch the failure mode instead of pretending your eyeballs do symbolic algebra.

Read the side-by-side like this

If the delayed force were harmless, both canvases would keep matching. They do not. As you lower cg, the delayed model’s force no longer points where a stable central force wants to point, so the Earth orbit radius and the Earth position difference drift away from the Newtonian one.
