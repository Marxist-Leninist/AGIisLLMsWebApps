Deterministic Path-Sum Raytracer

PROBABILITY DOESN'T EXIST (pilot-wave view): nothing in this engine is random. Standard renderers estimate light with random Monte Carlo samples. This one evaluates Feynman's sum over every point of the mirror with evenly spaced, deterministic samples, and refines it until it has converged.

QED: the picture is the one-photon amplitude for light polarised out of the screen. In 2D that amplitude obeys the wave equation exactly, with Green's function (i/4) H0(1)(kr), and a perfect mirror holds it at zero on its surface. The reflection is the sum over mirror points (physical-optics approximation: one bounce, perfect conductor). Where neighbouring paths have the same length they reinforce: that is where the classical “ray” comes from.

Pilot-wave paths (de Broglie–Bohm): each photon path is fixed by where it starts and follows the energy flow of its field (the Poynting vector). Paths never cross and steer around dark fringes. Averaged single-photon trajectories like these were measured in a 2011 two-slit experiment (Kocsis et al., Science).
